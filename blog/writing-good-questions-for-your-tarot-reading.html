Getting the most from your reading

Writing Good Questions for Your Tarot Reading

The question you bring shapes the reading you get. Here's how to write one that gets you more.

By Loveleen Sarao · 28 September 2026

The question you bring shapes the reading you get. Not because there's a trick to it, but because a clearer question gives the cards more to work with, and gives you more to take away.

You don't need a perfect question to book a reading. But if you've ever sat down to write yours and felt stuck, here's what tends to help.

Start with what's actually on your mind

Before you worry about phrasing, get clear on the real situation. A question usually comes from somewhere: a decision you're weighing, a relationship you're trying to understand, a pattern you keep noticing.

You can bring questions about relationships, career, life decisions, personal growth, or any other area where you'd like perspective. If you're not sure exactly what you want to ask, that's a fine place to begin too. Bringing "I'm not sure what I'm looking for, but here's what's going on" is a perfectly good opener.

Ask "what" or "how" before you ask "will" or "should"

This is the single biggest shift that makes a reading richer.

A yes-or-no question can only really be answered yes or no. A question that asks for a mechanism, a pattern or a next step gives the cards, and me, far more to explore with you.

  • Instead of "Will this relationship work out?" try "What's really going on between us right now?"
  • Instead of "Should I take the new job?" try "What would this move bring, and what would it cost me?"
  • Instead of "Will I be successful?" try "What's standing between me and what I want?"

None of this is about tricking the cards into a better answer. It's that "what" and "how" questions open a conversation, while "will" and "should" questions close one down to a single point.

Be specific rather than broad

"What does my future hold?" is a question with almost nowhere to land. "What should I focus on in my career this year?" gives a reading something concrete to speak to.

Specificity isn't about narrowing your options before you've even started. It's about giving your situation enough shape that a reading can actually meet it.

One question, one idea

If you're weighing two different things, that's two questions, not one folded into the other. "Should I leave my job, and is my relationship going to last?" is really two separate readings squeezed into one sentence.

This matters more for recorded readings, where each package gives you a set number of questions: 5, 7 or 11. Splitting a tangle of concerns into clear, separate questions means each one gets its own space, rather than one card trying to answer two things at once.

A little context goes a long way

You don't need to write an essay, but a sentence or two of background changes how a question can be read. "What should I focus on at work?" is open. "What should I focus on at work, now that I've been offered a promotion I'm unsure about?" gives a reading a foothold.

If you're booking a recorded reading, this is exactly what the optional note on the booking form is for. It's not required, but it's one of the easiest ways to get more out of your reading.

A few examples, side by side

Vague More useful
Will he come back? What's really going on in this relationship right now?
Should I quit my job? What would leaving actually change for me?
What does my future hold? What should I be paying attention to this year?
Am I on the right path? What patterns keep showing up in my choices lately?

Notice that the more useful versions aren't longer or more complicated. They're just aimed at something a reading can actually explore.

What if I still don't know what to ask?

That's alright. Not every question arrives fully formed, and a reading can help you find the shape of it. If you're bringing general guidance, rather than one specific question, that's a real and common way to book. You don't need to have it all worked out first.

Ready to bring your questions?

Whichever reading you choose, live or recorded, the same idea holds: a clearer question makes room for a clearer answer.
