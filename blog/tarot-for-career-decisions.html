Tarot for Career Decisions: What a Reading Can Offer

What a reading can offer, and what it can't, when a decision matters.

By Loveleen Sarao · 3 October 2026

Career questions tend to arrive at a crossroads: a job offer, a decision to stay or go, a stretch of feeling stuck with no clear next step. It's natural to want certainty before you choose.

A reading won't tell you which job to take or guarantee an outcome. What it can do is help you gain perspective on the direction, choices, opportunities and challenges in front of you, so the decision is clearer when you make it.

What a reading can actually help with

A reading can help you look at a decision from another angle, one you may not have had access to on your own. Not by telling you the "right" choice, but by helping you see what's actually weighing on you: which parts of the decision are about the job itself, and which are about fear, timing, or what you think you're supposed to do.

It can help with opportunities as much as obstacles. A new offer, a possible move, a path you haven't tried yet, a reading can help you consider what each option might actually ask of you, not just what it promises.

It can also be useful when nothing dramatic is happening at all. Sometimes the question isn't "should I leave?" but something quieter: what's making the day-to-day feel off, or what you're actually looking for in the next stage of your work.

What it won't do

It won't tell you which job to take. It won't guarantee a promotion, a successful interview, or that a particular path will work out. It won't make the decision smaller or safer than it actually is.

A reading that claimed to know which choice was "correct" wouldn't be offering you clarity, it would be offering you a guess dressed up as certainty. Career decisions usually involve real trade-offs, money, timing, other people, and no reading changes that. What it can do is help you meet those trade-offs with a clearer head.

A few examples of what to ask instead

As with any reading, the shape of the question changes what you get from it.

  • Instead of "Should I take the new job?" try "What would this move actually ask of me, and what would it cost me?"
  • Instead of "Will I get the promotion?" try "What's within my control here, and what isn't?"
  • Instead of "Is it time to leave?" try "What's actually making this feel unsustainable?"
  • Instead of "Will this business idea work?" try "What am I not seeing clearly about this yet?"

If you'd like more on this shift in general, this guide to writing good questions goes further into the "what" and "how" approach.

Why perspective still matters here

Career decisions are rarely only logical, even when they look that way on paper. They're tangled up with identity, security, other people's expectations, and a fair amount of guessing about a future nobody can actually see.

A reading doesn't resolve that uncertainty. It gives you a way to sit with it more clearly, so you're not making the decision from panic or from avoidance, but from an honest look at what you actually want and what you're actually afraid of.

Whether live or recorded suits this better

Career questions often benefit from talking it through, especially if you're weighing something layered, like a counteroffer, a career change, or a conflict at work, which can suit a live reading. If your question is more contained, or you'd rather reflect privately before deciding anything, a recorded reading works just as well.
