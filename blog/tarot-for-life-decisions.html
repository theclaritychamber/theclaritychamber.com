Tarot for Life Decisions: A Second View When a Choice Matters

A second view when a choice matters, without pretending to know the answer for you.

By Loveleen Sarao · 6 October 2026

Some decisions are loud: a relationship, a job. Others are quieter but just as heavy, moving cities, buying a home, starting a family, leaving somewhere familiar. When you're facing a choice like this, it's easy to feel like you need more information before you can decide. Often what you actually need is a clearer way of looking at the information you already have.

A reading can help you look at a decision from another angle, so you can decide for yourself. It is not financial or other professional advice, and it won't make the choice for you.

What a reading can actually help with

A reading can help you separate what's actually yours to decide from what's noise, pressure from other people, fear of getting it wrong, or simply the discomfort of choosing at all. Big decisions often feel harder than they are because several smaller feelings are tangled into one large one.

It can help you notice what you're weighting too heavily, and what you might be underweighting. Sometimes a decision feels stuck not because the options are unclear, but because one factor, money, timing, someone else's opinion, has quietly taken over the whole decision.

It can also be useful simply to slow down. Big decisions are often made in a rush, out of urgency or discomfort with uncertainty. A reading can give you a structured moment to actually sit with the choice before you make it.

What it won't do

It won't tell you which option is correct. It won't predict how a decision will turn out, or guarantee the outcome you're hoping for. And it is not financial, legal, medical or other professional advice, if your decision depends on numbers, contracts, health or law, that still needs the right professional alongside anything a reading offers.

A reading that claimed to know the "right" choice for a decision this size wouldn't be offering clarity. It would be offering false certainty about something genuinely uncertain. The decision, and the responsibility for it, stays yours.

A few examples of what to ask instead

  • Instead of "Should I move?" try "What am I actually hoping to find by moving, and would I find it?"
  • Instead of "Is this the right time?" try "What's making this feel urgent, and is that reason sound?"
  • Instead of "Will I regret this?" try "What am I most afraid of here, and is that fear accurate?"
  • Instead of "What should I do?" try "What haven't I let myself consider yet?"

If you'd like more on shaping a question like this, this guide to writing good questions goes further into the approach.

Why this still helps, without an answer

Big decisions rarely fail from a lack of information. They stall because the information is tangled up with fear, hope, other people's expectations, and the simple discomfort of choosing one path and closing off the others.

A reading doesn't remove that discomfort. It gives you a way to look at it directly instead of around it, so that whatever you decide, you're deciding from a clearer place rather than from pressure or avoidance.

Whether live or recorded suits this better

A decision with many moving parts, or one you're still finding the words for, often benefits from talking it through, which can suit a live reading. If your question is more contained, or you'd rather reflect privately before you decide anything, a recorded reading works just as well.
